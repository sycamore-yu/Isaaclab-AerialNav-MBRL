# PointNet-GRU static S0 training and encoder evidence

The full-attitude LiDAR run finished on 2026-11-04 at U8251 / 45,527,040 cumulative
transitions. Its final naturally completed training window contained 1026 episodes:
838 success (81.6764%), 143 collision (13.9376%), 12 bounds (1.1696%), 0 invalid
and 33 timeout (3.2164%). This is the first report above the stage threshold of
80% with at least 1024 episodes, not a held-out evaluation or retention study.
The preceding four windows were 77.7344%, 77.6265%, 79.6296%, and 77.8426%.

The final continuation used 32 env, H160, 240 static / 0 dynamic obstacles,
no randomization, body-frame XYZ, PointNet-GRU192, and three acceleration outputs.
It retained learned state from a saved U452 boundary and a 128-env U457 boundary;
it was not a fresh-start run. The 32-env process executed 7794 further updates
in 14.4226 hours. Median time over the final ten updates was 5.0626 s on a shared
RTX 4090. No nonfinite state or gradient events were recorded. Final, stage and
best-stage checkpoints exist; the training process exited normally.

## Encoder comparison retained from the optimization study

Same U24 checkpoint, 128 env, H160, shared GPU:

| Update | Dense FP32 / microbatch16 | Packed BF16 compiled / microbatch64 | Dense loss | Candidate loss |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| U25 | 27.8865 s | 19.3872 s including cold compilation | 3.656755 | 3.656806 |
| U26 | 30.1819 s | 9.3879 s | 2.975142 | 2.968674 |

Both completed with zero nonfinite events and saved checkpoints. The candidate
preserved all valid returns and used 320 encoder calls per update. Peak Torch
allocation was 1452.04 MiB versus 3442.29 MiB, while reserved memory was higher:
9216 MiB versus 4444 MiB. BF16 is a rounding change, not exact FP32 equivalence.
These two updates do not establish isolated-GPU speedup or training convergence.
Triton did not beat the selected compiled PyTorch encoder and is not the default.
